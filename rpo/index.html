RPO

Routing Policy Optimization for online diffusion reinforcement learning.

Reinforcement learning can align visual generators with rewards beyond pretraining, but score-function methods usually inject noise into the reverse trajectory and pay for it with large groups of complete rollouts. RPO moves exploration inside the generator: attention routing becomes a discrete policy, and inference returns to dense attention.

01 · The idea

Put the randomness inside attention, not on the sampler's path.

Sampler-space RL such as Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO explores by perturbing the reverse process, then estimates an advantage from a group of complete stochastic rollouts.

RPO keeps the deterministic sampler. At one denoising level, each query block keeps a random subset of its candidate key blocks, with Bernoulli probabilities taken from the generator's own query–key affinities. The sampled masks are the actions.

Denoise densely

Run the reverse process as usual down to a sampled routed level. The sampler is untouched.

02

Draw two masks

Safety blocks stay on. Each candidate block is kept with its Bernoulli probability.

03

Finish the pair

Same prompt, latent, level and layers. Only the sampled routes differ.

04

Score the difference

The reward difference becomes the advantage for the recorded routing actions.

05

Update shared weights

A replay likelihood gradient updates the attention parameters dense inference reuses.

Nothing changes at inference. All masks are removed: dense attention and the original deterministic sampler. The gains transfer because the routing policy reuses the generator's native attention parameters.

02 · Results

Faster to learn, and the gain survives dense inference.

Training rewards are scored on routed or SDE rollouts, so the comparison that matters is the dense model that ships: same prompts, same noise, same sampler.

Dense evaluation gain · Wan2.2, HPSv3 reward
Markers are values stated in the paper's text; lines between them are schematic. Gain is measured against each method's own step-0 score.

RPO reaches its final gain within about 15 steps and keeps it. It gains +2.17 by step 5 and about +2.9 by step 15, then stays near +3 through step 50.

Flow-GRPO follows its training reward. The dense model dips to about −3 at step 10 and only recovers to +2.7 by step 50, so it needs the whole 50-step window to approach the level RPO reaches within about 15.

DanceGRPO accumulates almost no gain, peaking at +0.29 at step 5 and staying between about 0 and +0.2.

RPO collects 64 matched pairs per step and routes a single denoising level; DanceGRPO uses groups of G = 8 and updates the full model.

The paper's table lists the eight VBench dimensions where RPO exceeds both RL baselines, plus the overall score. Other dimensions show trade-offs, described alongside.

RPO is the only trained checkpoint above the base model overall (82.00 against 81.61; DanceGRPO 81.46, Flow-GRPO 81.19). Differences from the base are largest on semantic dimensions: human action +4.80, object class +4.40, scene +3.43 and multiple objects +2.15, and smaller on quality and consistency dimensions.

There are trade-offs. RPO lowers color and appearance style relative to the base model, although it still leads the trained baselines there. The four Wan2.2 overall scores span only 0.81 points on one seed set, so the aggregate ranking is directional.
